Ready to Sell a Card?

Here's the whole game plan, start to finish — no computer experience needed. Six steps. Take your time on each one.

0 of 6 steps done

Before You Start

1
Create Your eBay Account
5 minutes

If you already have an eBay account you use for buying, you can use that same one to sell — you don't need a separate account.

If you don't have one yet:

  1. Go to eBay.com (or open the eBay app).
  2. Look for the button that says Register or Sign Up, usually top-right of the screen.
  3. Enter your name, email, and a password. eBay will send a confirmation email — click the link inside it.
Tip: Use an email address you check often — this is how eBay tells you when someone buys, messages, or pays.
Go to eBay.com →
2
Set Up How You Get Paid
5 minutes

Before your first listing, eBay wants to know where to send your money. This is a one-time setup.

  1. On eBay.com, go to your Account or Seller Hub settings.
  2. Look for a section about getting paid or payout options.
  3. Add your bank account or debit card details. eBay may ask you to confirm your identity — this is normal and just protects you.
Don't worry: eBay walks you through this with its own on-screen instructions. If a screen looks confusing, look for a "Learn more" or "?" link right on that page — eBay explains everything as you go.
3
Scan the Card
1 minute per card

This is where the Big Blue Scanner does the hard part for you. It looks at your card and writes the listing title, the description, and even suggests a price — you don't have to write any of it yourself.

  1. Open the Scanner page (button below).
  2. Tap the Front box and take a clear, well-lit photo of the front of the card.
  3. Optional: tap the Back box too — it helps accuracy.
  4. Tap Appraise Card and wait a few seconds.
Tip: Lay the card flat on a plain surface (a table works fine) with good light. Avoid glare and shadows across the card.
Open the Scanner →
4
Copy the Title & Description
30 seconds

After the scanner finishes, it shows you an eBay title box and an eBay description box, each with a small Copy button next to it.

  1. Tap Copy next to the title. It's now saved on your phone, ready to paste.
  2. Tap Copy next to the description too.
  3. Also note the Estimated Value shown — you'll use that as a starting price in the next step.
You'll paste these into eBay's listing form in the next step — no typing required.
5
Create the Listing on eBay
5 minutes

Now switch over to the eBay app or eBay.com to actually put the card up for sale.

  1. Tap the button that says Sell (usually a big button in the eBay app, or in the menu on eBay.com).
  2. When it asks for a title, paste the title you copied in Step 4 (press and hold the text box, then tap Paste).
  3. Do the same for the description.
  4. Take 2–4 real photos of the card directly in the eBay listing form — front, back, and a close-up of any scratches or wear. Good photos matter more than anything else for selling fast.
  5. Set your price. The scanner's Estimated Value is a solid starting point — you can price at that number or slightly below if you want it to sell quicker.
  6. Choose a shipping option. A cheap, trackable option (eBay will suggest one) works well for single cards.
  7. Review everything, then tap List Item (or Publish).
Tip: Slide the card into a penny sleeve before photographing it — it looks more professional and protects it right away.
6
Ship It & Get Paid
once it sells

eBay will send you an email or app notification the moment someone buys your card. Here's what happens next:

  1. eBay will let you print a shipping label right from the app or website — this is usually the easiest, cheapest way to ship.
  2. Pack the card safely: a penny sleeve, then a rigid card holder or a few pieces of cardboard, in a small envelope or bubble mailer. Cards should never bend.
  3. Drop the package off at the post office (or your carrier's drop box) and mark the order as shipped in the eBay app.
  4. Once the buyer receives it with no issues, eBay deposits the money into the bank account you set up in Step 2 — usually within a couple of business days.
That's it — you just made your first sale. Every card after this one gets faster and easier.

Quick Words to Know

Listing
The page you create that shows your card for sale — title, photos, description, and price.
Buyer
The person who purchases your card.
Shipping Label
A sticker you print and put on the package — it tells the carrier where to send it.
Tracking Number
A code that lets you and the buyer see where the package is on its way.
Feedback
A short rating a buyer can leave after a purchase — good feedback helps future buyers trust you.

A Few Tips for Faster Sales

  • Good photos sell cards. Natural light near a window, plain background, no glare.
  • Be honest about condition. If there's a bent corner or a scratch, say so and show it in a photo — buyers trust honest listings and it avoids returns.
  • Price close to the scanner's estimate. Pricing far above it means it may sit unsold; pricing well below it means leaving money on the table.
  • Answer buyer questions quickly. A fast reply often turns a question into a sale.
  • Never share your eBay password with anyone, even someone claiming to be eBay support.
